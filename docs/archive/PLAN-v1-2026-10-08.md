# ChallengeForge — product & build plan

**Status:** plan only, no code. Drafted 2026-10-08.
**Stack decided:** Node.js + MySQL, self-hostable (Hostinger Node hosting works).
**Relationship to existing work:** the Clinical AI Challenge Lab is *harvested*
into this platform as the first challenge pack and as the reusable engine — not
migrated in place.

---

## 1. What we are building

A platform that does for **interactive challenges** what WordPress did for
websites: let a non-engineer **create, grade, and publish** them, and let others
**host their own copy** or run on a shared one.

A "challenge" here is anything with: some material to engage with, something the
learner **does** (answers, or works through a scenario), and an **automatic,
trustworthy assessment** of how they did — optionally with hints, scoring, a
leaderboard, and a certificate.

**The platform is domain-agnostic.** It is not a security tool, a medical tool,
or a coding tool — it is the *scaffolding* any of those sit on. A few of the many
shapes it should support, deliberately spanning fields:

- **Medical diagnostic simulations** — present a patient; the learner takes a
  history, orders investigations, interprets results, reaches a diagnosis and a
  management plan; the platform grades the *reasoning path*, not just the final
  answer. (OSCE-style cases, "work up this patient", image/slide spot-diagnosis.)
- **Clinical reasoning / case quizzes** — structured questions over a case.
- **Classic CTF** — jeopardy flags, for the security-teaching audience.
- **Data-audit or spreadsheet exercises** — find the error in a dataset.
- **Code-review / "spot the bug" drills.**
- **AI-interaction missions** — interview a simulated patient, or red-team an AI.

What unifies them is not the subject but the **two paradigms of challenge** the
platform must handle:

1. **Submit-and-assess** (static): the learner produces an answer or artefact; it
   is checked once. (Flags, multiple choice, numeric targets, free-text graded by
   rubric or AI.)
2. **Interactive scenario** (stateful): the learner takes a *sequence* of actions
   and the challenge responds — a case that unfolds, a simulated patient or tool
   that reacts — and the whole **trajectory and outcome** are assessed. Medical
   simulations and AI missions live here.

Designing for both from day one is the difference between "a CTF app" and "a
platform". The engine must treat a challenge as a small lifecycle —
**present → interact/collect → evaluate → score & feedback** — where the simple
submit-and-assess case is just the one-step version of the interactive one.

So the platform cares about: **authoring (by domain experts, not engineers),
running challenges of both paradigms, assessing them trustworthily, accounts,
hosting, and publishing** — and stays out of the way of the subject.

## 2. The key decision: standalone platform, **not** a WordPress plugin

You asked whether this should be a WordPress plugin. **Recommendation: build a
standalone Node + MySQL platform, and offer a thin WordPress *bridge* later —
not a plugin.** Reasons:

- **WordPress is PHP.** A real WP plugin means writing the grading engine, the
  LLM gateway and the anti-cheat logic in PHP, inside WP's request model. That
  throws away everything reusable in the current TypeScript codebase — the exact
  opposite of "import our current work smartly."
- **The valuable parts don't fit WP.** Server-side answer-checking that the
  learner can't see or tamper with, streaming AI missions, per-user budget caps —
  these want a real application server, not WP's page-and-hook model.
- **You'd inherit WP's baggage** (its security surface, its update treadmill,
  its data model) for no benefit to *this* problem.

**But the WordPress-like *qualities* are worth copying** — that is the real
insight in your idea:

| WordPress quality | What it becomes here |
|---|---|
| Install it yourself on cheap hosting | One-command / wizard install on Node + MySQL |
| Themes change the look | **Themes** for the player and author UI |
| Plugins add behaviour | **Challenge-type packs** + **extensions** (the core idea, §3) |
| Custom post types | **Challenge types** registered by packs |
| wp-admin | An **author/admin dashboard** |
| The plugin/theme directory | A **pack registry** (much later) |

And for people who *do* live on WordPress, a later lightweight bridge lets them
**embed a ChallengeForge challenge** in a WP page and sign in with their WP
account — integration without being a plugin.

## 3. The core idea: challenge-type packs

The one architectural bet that makes this a *platform* and not just "the Lab on
MySQL": **a challenge is defined by data + a pluggable *type*, and the Clinical
AI content becomes pack #1.**

- A **challenge type** plugs into the lifecycle (present → interact/collect →
  evaluate → score) and declares: what the author fills in, how the learner
  interacts, how the result is assessed, how it is scored, and how it is shown.
  It names which paradigm it is (submit-and-assess or interactive scenario).
  Examples across both paradigms: `flag`, `multiple-choice`, `set-match`,
  `metric-target`, `free-text-rubric` (static); `diagnostic-sim`,
  `branching-case`, `simulated-patient`, `ai-redteam` (interactive).
- A **pack** is a bundle of challenge definitions + the content (text, data
  files, images, answer keys / rubrics). "Clinical AI Challenge — 2026 Edition"
  is one pack; "Diagnostic Cases in Paediatrics" could be another.
- **Authoring is for domain experts, not engineers** — a doctor builds a
  diagnostic case by filling in a structured form (presentation, available
  investigations and their results, the correct diagnosis and management, the
  scoring rubric) the way a blogger writes a post. This is the real
  "WordPress for challenges" promise. Power users can still author as files;
  packs export/import to move content between installations.

The current Lab already has **both paradigms** — its static missions and its
Level 3 live-AI chat — just hardwired to clinical content. That is why the import
is *smart*, not a rewrite (see §5), and why the interactive paradigm is proven,
not speculative.

## 4. Architecture

A **monorepo** of small packages, so the reusable engine is shared and the
platform stays maintainable:

```
challengeforge/
  packages/
    engine/          # grading, scoring, flag/answer validators — PURE logic, no DB
    content-schema/  # the challenge/pack definition format + validation (Zod)
    llm-gateway/     # multi-provider AI calls, budget caps, BYOK — reusable service
    db/              # MySQL data layer: repositories + migrations (the only SQL)
    auth/            # self-built accounts, sessions, roles
    web/             # the Next.js app: player UI + author/admin dashboard
  packs/
    clinical-ai/     # the first challenge pack (harvested content)
  apps/
    installer/       # setup wizard / CLI
```

Design rules that avoid the Postgres-specific pain the Lab had:

- **All authorisation and atomicity live in the app layer by design** — not in
  the database. (Postgres row-level security and stored procedures were doing
  that in the Lab; here we build it into `engine`/`db` from day one, so MySQL's
  lack of them is a non-issue, not a gap.)
- **The engine package is pure and database-free** — it takes a submission and
  an answer key and returns a verdict. That makes it trivially testable and
  reusable, and keeps answer-keys server-side.
- **The data layer is the only place that knows it is MySQL** — swap-able later.

## 5. Harvesting the current Lab (the "smart import")

This is the part that makes the whole plan efficient. Mapping what exists to
where it lands:

| In the Lab today | Fate | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| `src/server/validators/*` (exact, set-match, numeric-range, metric-target, llm-rubric, reproduce, canary, test-suite) | **Lift almost as-is** — already generic answer-checkers | `packages/engine` |
| `src/engine/*` (content, curriculum, schema, flag-rules, scoring, modules, assessment) | **Lift & generalise** — strip "clinical" assumptions, keep the model | `packages/engine` + `content-schema` |
| `src/server/llm/*` (gateway, providers, caps, canary, BYOK credentials) | **Lift & re-point storage** to the new data layer | `packages/llm-gateway` |
| The React UI (player, mission pages, dashboards) | **Reuse as a theme** — re-skin, remove clinical hardcoding | `packages/web` |
| Auth, payments, institutions/cohorts, certificates | **Re-implement as platform features** on MySQL (new, not migrated) | `auth`, `web`, `db` |
| Supabase data layer, RLS policies, stored procedures | **Do not migrate** — replaced by `db` + app-layer authz | — |
| The clinical content (YAML challenges, answer keys, modules) | **Becomes pack #1** | `packs/clinical-ai` |
| The ~700 unit tests (esp. validator/scoring tests) | **Harvest** — they test the pure engine and move with it | `packages/engine` tests |

Net effect: the genuinely hard, proven, well-tested logic (checking answers,
scoring, running AI missions safely) is **reused**. The parts that were tied to
Supabase (storage, auth, DB-enforced rules) are **rebuilt fresh on MySQL** — and
since we're building new, there's no "un-wiring," just clean construction.

## 6. Data model on MySQL (native)

Designed for MySQL 8 from the start, with authz and atomicity handled in app
code (not DB features):

- **Accounts & orgs:** users, sessions, organisations, memberships, roles.
- **Content:** packs, challenges, challenge_versions, assets.
- **Play:** attempts, submissions, scores, hints_used, events.
- **AI:** llm_usage, llm_credentials (encrypted), budgets.
- **Commerce (optional):** access_grants, payments, certificates.

The race-sensitive operations the Lab solved in Postgres functions (grade a
submission once; don't overspend the AI budget; don't double-claim a seat) are
built here as **MySQL transactions with row locks**, designed in from day one
and covered by the harvested integrity tests. No surprises, because we're not
discovering them mid-migration — they're on the plan.

## 7. Hosting target and the one real caveat

- Runs on **Hostinger Node hosting + MySQL** (your Business plan), or any Node
  host. No PHP, no Docker required.
- **The one honest caveat, carried over:** AI challenge types make long,
  streaming calls. Shared hosting can cut long requests short. The platform
  should support a **non-streaming fallback** for AI missions so it runs
  everywhere, and streaming only where the host allows it. Flagging this now so
  it's designed in, not discovered late.

## 8. MVP, then roadmap

Resist building the whole platform at once. A grounded sequence:

| Phase | Goal | What "done" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Spike** | Prove the engine is reusable | `packages/engine` extracted from the Lab, its tests passing, with zero clinical assumptions and zero database. |
| **1. MVP (single pack, single tenant)** | One site that hosts the Clinical AI pack on MySQL | Accounts, author dashboard for one challenge type (flag/answer), play + grade + score, Clinical AI Level 1 imported, deployed on Hostinger. |
| **2. The pack system + both paradigms** | More than one challenge type, and prove the *interactive* paradigm | Challenge-type plugin interface; at least one static type and **one interactive type — a `diagnostic-sim`** authored through a form by a non-engineer; export/import a pack. This is where the platform stops being "a CTF app". |
| **3. Publishing & commerce** | Others can author and (optionally) charge | Full author dashboard, certificates, payments, cohorts/orgs. |
| **4. Multi-site & themes** | "Like WordPress" | One install hosts many challenge sites; themeable UI. |
| **5. Registry & WP bridge** | Ecosystem | A pack directory; embed + SSO for WordPress sites. |

Phases 0–1 are the real proof. Everything after is additive and only worth doing
if 1 finds an audience.
